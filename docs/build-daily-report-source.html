03

What was built

The forced scroll on the contractor agreement is gone Closed

Was
A contractor could not tick “I agree” until they had scrolled to the last line of the terms. On a phone that is a long way, and it taught people to flick to the bottom without reading.
Now
The tick is available immediately. Nothing about the record weakens — the server still records who signed, which version the database chose, the name they typed, and a timestamped activity row.

One thing was added that was not asked for: switching the document or the language now clears the tick and the typed name. Without it somebody could agree to the English terms, switch to French, and submit a signature against text nobody had read.

Documents open in the app Closed

Was
Every document could only be downloaded. To look at one certificate you downloaded a file, found it, opened it in another application, and came back.
Now
Filenames are clickable and there is a view button beside Download on both the desktop and mobile rows. Download is untouched for anybody who wants the file.

Project money is shown in CFA Francs Closed

Was
Every client money screen printed US dollars.
Now
A project shows its own currency throughout — Overview, Budget, Stages, the Payments ledger, the pre-tracking gate and the exported budget PDF. One project, one currency, one exchange rate, held in one place so two panels on the same screen can never disagree.

This did not re-open the exchange-rate question. The live-rate mechanism already existed — built on 29 October for the contractor Work screens, fetching a real rate and falling back to the stored one. The fix was to point the client screens at something we already had.

Checked end to end. A Cameroon project stored at $44,480 renders 26,688,000 XAF. The real bill of quantities for that building is 26,687,728 XAF. Those agree to within a rounding step.

The outstanding balance was wrong — root cause, not a patch Closed

Was
The Overview showed outstanding as total budget minus what had been paid. On an unpaid project it printed the whole budget twice, and it could never reach zero even on a fully paid one.
Now
Both sides are added up from the actual obligations — the stages plus the fee records. Planned = Paid + Outstanding now holds, and it reaches zero when the project is settled.

Why it happened. Stage milestones only cover construction and design. The permit, professional, verification and contingency fees are separate records attached to no stage at all. Subtracting from the grand total therefore left those four fees stranded in “outstanding” forever.

A second defect in the same card, which nobody had reported Closed

While fixing the above I found that the per-category paid bars — how much of Materials, Labour, Permit and Design had been paid — were not read from anything that had been paid. They were produced by running the pricing formula backwards over the paid total.

The practical effect: pay the first construction milestone, and the permit bar fills in proportionally, reporting a permit fee as partly paid when no permit had been paid at all. Those figures now come from the actual stage and fee records. If no permit has been paid, the permit bar is empty.

Contractors and Messages are off the client menu Closed

The client sidebar goes from nine items to seven, which also reshuffles the mobile tab bar. The dashboard was duplicating both doors, so the Contractors card is gone and the Messages card now appears only when there is a build for it to open.

The routes stay registered. Notification emails, the bell’s fallback and anyone’s bookmarks already point at both, and a dead link is a worse answer than a page nothing signposts. This removes the signposting, not the destination.

A small side benefit: dropping the Contractors card also removed a database count query that ran on every dashboard load and now renders nothing.

Floors can be corrected on the summary Closed

Was
Editable in exactly one situation — the document upload path, and only when reading the plan had failed.
Now
Editable on both paths, with the destructive case handled.

The two cases that actually matter were both missing. First, extraction succeeding but being wrong — the floor count is parsed out of a bill title like “Mr.NAS G + 1”, and a ground-floor plan showing a staircase states no storey count at all. Second, the ordinary path, where floors were buried inside a joined “Scale” line and could not be touched.

One catch had to be handled. Step 4 of the wizard deals with a floor change by clearing every room. That is harmless there, because rooms are asked for on the very next screen. On the summary it would have thrown away the longest piece of work in the whole wizard because somebody corrected 2 to 3. The new shared helper resizes instead: existing floors keep their rooms, new floors start empty, and the room totals the budget is priced from are recalculated in the same step.

The invited client can now see their own house Closed

This was the largest single finding of the week, and nobody had reported it — because it produces no error anywhere.

A client reaches Groundwork two ways. Self-created: they signed up, ran the wizard, and own the project record. Invited: their contractor set the build up — the normal case we designed for — so the contractor owns the record and the client is invited onto it.

Every client screen asked the database for “projects where the owner is me”. So the invited client signed in and the dashboard, My Builds, Documents and Payments all told them they had no builds and offered to help them start one — on the platform their own house was being tracked on. The only thing that worked for them was the deep link in their invitation email.

The permissions were never the problem; the database has returned that project to that client since migration 112. The question the screens asked was wrong.

Self-createdInvited
First quick action Upload Updates Progress Updates
New Project buttons Shown (three on the dashboard, two on the list) Withheld
Profile completion tracker Shown Withheld
Build card Status only “Shared with you”, and why

Each has a reason behind it. An invited client cannot file a site update — the server refuses them by name — so offering them an Upload button is offering an action that will fail. They have no project to create and never will, so a tracker whose last item is “First project created” would sit at 67% permanently, telling them to undo their own onboarding.

The read-only note on the build is deliberate. The alternative is a client who is paying for a house working out from absent buttons that something is broken. One sentence — approvals and payments stay with whoever is running the build — answers it.

Note the test. A client who has one build of their own and one shared with them is an ordinary client and keeps everything. Only somebody whose builds are all somebody else’s is on the invited path.

Qualified-projects tracking corrected migration 182 Closed

The definition confirmed on 9 November is that a verified project is a qualified project with both sides active on it. The live rule did not check it that way — it asked only whether a client had joined and something had been posted from site, and never looked at the bill of quantities or the start date.

I reproduced both versions side by side on a local PostgreSQL 16 database against real rows. That turned up two ways into a false “verified”, not the one I expected.

ProjectOld ruleNew rule
Client joined + site update, no bill of quantities Verified General
BQ + client + site update, start date 90 days out Verified General
Everything in place Verified Verified
BQ + near start date only Qualified Qualified

The second is the dangerous one and the one I had not anticipated: the project looks complete from every angle except the date. The function and the bulk view now agree on all four cases, which they did not have to — the 30 October meeting opened with exactly those two numbers disagreeing with each other.

Production reads 3 qualified, 0 verified, so no number we have reported has ever been wrong. That is precisely why it is worth fixing this week. The target is two verified projects, and the first project to pick up a client and a site update without a bill of quantities on file would have been counted as verified and would not have been one. A metric that is only correct while its count is zero is not yet a metric.

05

Decisions waiting on you

The floor plan requirement — my recommendation Needs you

You asked me to evaluate whether the floor plan is necessary. It is already optional, and its only load-bearing output is now two numbers a person can type.

The document path requires both a floor plan and a bill of quantities. Of everything we read off the drawing, only the floor count and the building footprint affect anything — and as of today both are editable on the summary.

So requiring the drawing buys accuracy, not possibility. I would relax the gate to require the bill of quantities and make the floor plan optional. The BQ is what the pricing is actually checked against, and a client who has the bill but not stamped drawings is currently pushed onto the estimate-only path for no reason.

That is a product decision, not a cleanup, so I have not touched it.

Session recording and heat mapping — vendor not chosen Needs you

This needs a decision before any code. Microsoft Clarity is free, Hotjar is the familiar one, PostHog can be self-hosted.

There is a privacy dimension that is not optional: these tools record client financial screens — budgets, payment amounts, document names — so whichever we pick needs field masking configured before it goes anywhere near production, and the privacy policy needs the sub-processor named. Tell me which one and I will wire it with masking on from the first commit.

The client dashboard — what does “redesign” mean? Needs you

The dashboard structure is now correct for both kinds of client. I have not done a visual redesign: our screen-design record has the dashboard as variant B, signed off on 9 August, with its hero and costing donut already built to that decision.

If “redesign” means something beyond the invited / self-created split, it needs a mockup or a few sentences. I would rather ask than repaint a screen that was approved.

06

Risks to clear before the next deployment

These are not today’s work. They are known hazards that should not be discovered during a deploy.

  1. Two migrations fight over the contractor directory — verified today

    Migration 180 opens the contractor directory to every signed-in account, per your own words on 9 November: the directory should be admin-curated, not subscription-gated. A second file, 20261109000000_free_access_and_locked_directory, redefines the same database function to admins only.

    Migrations run in filename order, and 180… sorts before 2026…. So the later file wins and the directory stays shut to everybody except staff — the opposite of what 180 was written to do. 180’s own header already flags this.

    Fix: renumber the two timestamped files into the 18x sequence, or apply them by hand in the intended order. One line of naming, but it has to happen before deploy.

  2. Migrations 174 and 175 collide with free mode

    174 makes the client the approver of a stage; 20261109001000_remove_verification_gates switches verification off entirely. They have not been reconciled. Neither should ship until they have been.

  3. “Record a payment” writes to the browser only

    On the Payments page, the Record button saves to local browser storage under a comment reading “no payments table yet”. There has been a payments table since migration 090.

    So that button records nothing the admin can see, nothing that survives a different browser or device, and nothing that reconciles against the ledger — and it sits on the page next to real figures. It is either a wiring job or a deletion. Your call; I have left it alone.

  4. The wizard contradicts the Cameroon default

    A fresh wizard starts with no country selected, added on 28 October so that step 1 reflects an explicit choice. Our standing rule is that Cameroon is the default everywhere. Two of the nine failing tests are reporting this honestly rather than being broken.

    Both positions are defensible. It is a product call, so I have not changed it, and the two tests stay red until somebody makes it.

  5. Migration 104 remains on hold

    Still held, with the two blocking defects recorded previously: no transaction wrapper, and no repair pass before the constraints are applied.
