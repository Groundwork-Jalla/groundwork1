Start here

Getting in, and reading the furniture

Two things to know before the tour: the admin has its own door, and its own permanent frame.

01

Signing in

Go to www.tryjalla.com/admin/login

This is a separate door from the one clients use. There is no “sign up” and no Google button, deliberately: a staff account is only ever created by another member of staff. The page says “This area is restricted to Groundwork staff.”

If you land on the ordinary client login instead, check the address — it must end in /admin/login. Signing in at /auth/login with a staff account works too and will route you to the admin, but the restricted door is the one to bookmark.

If two-factor authentication is on for your account you will be asked for a code before you get in.

02

The shell: sidebar, sections, top bar

Every admin page sits in the same frame, so once you can read it you can read all of them.

The left sidebar is grouped into eight labelled sections — Overview, Work, Finance, People, Communication, Acquisition, Support, Analytics and System. The grouping is the mental model: you are never hunting alphabetically, you are asking “is this about money, or about people?”

The top bar carries the page title, a search box covering projects and people, a language toggle (EN/FR — the whole admin is translated), a dark-mode toggle, a WhatsApp shortcut, a + New button and the notification bell. At the bottom of the sidebar: your name, language, dark mode and log out.

Dark mode is remembered against your account, not the browser, so it follows you between devices.

Four menu items are not built yet

Inspections, Tasks, Site Managers and Agents are in the menu but open an honest “not built yet” page rather than a fake one. Section 21 covers this. Everything else in the menu is real.

Overview

Where you land, and what is waiting

These two pages answer “how is the business doing?” and “what needs me today?” — in that order.

03

Overview

Go to www.tryjalla.com/admin

The landing page. It greets you by name and dates itself, then shows eight live counts:

CardWhat it counts
Total projectsEverything not archived
Pending reviewsStages awaiting your approval
Total usersEvery account on Groundwork
Contractor applicationsAwaiting a decision
Quote requestsIntroductions still to make
Needs replyClients waiting on us to answer
Projects at riskDerived from live state — not a flag anyone sets
Pending budgetsManagement builds awaiting a budget

Every card is a filtered dataset, not a decoration — clicking one takes you to the list it counted. Below the cards: a map shaded by real project counts per country (no invented pins), contractors by trade, the applications funnel by real status, recent activity from the audit log, open support tickets and the GoHighLevel integration state.

04

Action Center

Go to www.tryjalla.com/admin/action-center

The single queue of everything waiting on the desk, ranked Critical / High / Medium / Low. Typical items: a project with no verifier assigned, an application to decide, a stage awaiting funding, an open quote request, a client waiting on a reply.

The important property, stated on the page itself: it is “computed from live state on each load — nothing is stored and nothing can be dismissed.” There is no “mark as done”. An item leaves the list when the underlying fact changes — you assign the verifier, you decide the application. That is deliberate: a dismissable queue drifts away from reality within a week.

Each row shows who and which project it concerns, and how long it has been waiting.

Work

The builds themselves

Projects, the workspace where one is run, the approval queue, and what site has reported.

05

Projects

Go to www.tryjalla.com/admin/projects

Every project on Groundwork, whoever owns it: project name, owner, tier (Self Verify / Jalla Verify / Jalla Management), status, stage as 5 / 10, and created date.

Create for a client, top right, runs the full eleven-step build questionnaire on a client's behalf and produces a Jalla Management project owned by them. Row actions let you open the workspace, assign a contractor, or delete.

06

The project workspace

Go to www.tryjalla.com/admin/projects/<project id>

Where a single build is actually run. A header strip carries the client, plan, stage position, tracking state and last activity, plus the actions: Assign verifier, Jalla message, WhatsApp, and Open client view (see it as the client sees it).

Seven tabs: Overview · Stages · Financials · Site updates · Documents · Conversations · Activity. Stages is where you approve or request changes; Financials is this project's slice of the ledger; Activity is the audit trail for this project alone.

07

Reviews & Approvals

Go to www.tryjalla.com/admin/reviews

The approval queue: Jalla Verify stages submitted and waiting on you. Each card expands to the stage's substages with the evidence attached to each — “Evidence 1, Evidence 2” open the real files through signed links, and substages with nothing yet say “No evidence uploaded” rather than looking done.

Below that, any site updates filed against the stage, and an Independent verification panel. When no verifier is assigned it says so plainly and tells you where to fix it.

Two actions: Request changes and Approve stage. Approving is what unlocks the next stage and its milestone.

This is where the verification boundary shows

A verifier records an independent finding; only an admin approves the stage. If a verifier is assigned, their finding appears here as evidence for your decision — it does not make the decision.

08

Site Updates

Go to www.tryjalla.com/admin/site-updates

What has been reported from site, across every project, newest first — who filed it, when, how many files, and the note they wrote. Filter by project and by person.

This is the raw feed from contractors on the ground. The same updates appear inside each project's workspace; this page is for scanning everything at once.

Finance

Money: the plan, and what happened

Budgets and fees are the plan. The ledger is what actually moved. Groundwork records and governs it; a licensed payment provider holds and moves the funds.

Read this before judging the money screens

Groundwork does not hold client money. It records what has been funded, authorises each milestone release, and tracks what the provider did. You will not find the words escrow or wallet anywhere, and that is deliberate rather than an oversight.

09

Finance overview

Go to www.tryjalla.com/admin/finance

Four figures across the top — Confirmed inflows, Approved for release, Disbursed, and Available project funding (what is left after approved releases). Then Recent money movement: every inflow and disbursement with project, stage, amount, state and date.

Money in and money out read differently on purpose. Inflows are Funding confirmed; outflows move through Release approved → Processing → Paid.

10

Inflows

Go to www.tryjalla.com/admin/finance/inflows

Money arriving from clients, against the stage it funds. Totals for Confirmed received and Expected funding, a movement count, and tabs for Received and Expected. Each row names the client, the project and stage, the amount, and the confirmation date.

11

Disbursements

Go to www.tryjalla.com/admin/finance/disbursements

Money leaving to contractors, against the work it pays for. Four states across the top — Release approved, Processing, Paid, and Payment issue — then the rows, each naming the contractor, project, stage and amount.

“Payment issue” is a real failure state reported back by the provider, not a warning badge.

12

Budgets & fees

Go to www.tryjalla.com/admin/budgets

Two jobs on one page. At the top, Budget Confirmations — Jalla Management projects awaiting a confirmed budget before tracking can begin, each with the estimate and a Set budget action. Until a budget is set, the build cannot start its stages.

Below, Financial operations: where the money of each build stands, with “what needs a person comes first”. Totals for Funded, Expected, Release authorised, In transit and Disbursed, then a per-project breakdown. Projects with nothing recorded say “nothing recorded yet” rather than showing zeroes.

People

Everyone Groundwork works with

Four lists over the same records: clients, contractors, verifiers, and every account.

Admin manages the actor; admin does not become the actor

These pages are management and oversight — identity, status, assignments, projects, activity, and the actions staff may take. They are not the screens those people use to do their jobs. A contractor works at /work and a verifier at /verifiers; you never need to sign in there to manage them.

13

Clients

Go to www.tryjalla.com/admin/clients

The people Groundwork builds for, and how their builds are going. Each card: name, email, when they joined, when they were last seen, and counts for Projects, Conversations and Open tickets — then their projects listed with stage, tier, status and location.

Two honest states worth recognising: “never signed in” against someone provisioned but not yet active, and “No project yet — this account has signed up but not started a build.”

14

Contractors

Go to www.tryjalla.com/admin/contractors

The published directory clients browse — not everyone who applied. Entries appear here when an application is accepted, which is the single route in. Tabs for All and Hidden; hiding an entry keeps its history and is reversible, deleting is not.

An empty directory says “No contractors published yet. Accept an application to add one.” — which tells you the fix rather than just the fact.

15

Verifiers

Go to www.tryjalla.com/admin/verifiers

Who can verify, in what discipline, and what they are carrying now. Each person shows their profile — disciplines, registration body and number where recorded — plus Projects, Pending and Decided counts and when they last decided something.

Someone holding the role without a profile is shown as “No verifier profile yet — they hold the role but have recorded no disciplines.” Assignment happens from a project workspace, and the page says so.

16

Users

Go to www.tryjalla.com/admin/users

Every account on Groundwork in one table — name, email, role (Client / Contractor / Verifier / Admin), plan, project count, joined date. Search by name, email or role.

Create a client account provisions a Jalla Management client and shows you a temporary password once to hand over. An account created that way and not yet used is marked “Awaiting first sign-in”, which clears itself when they choose their own password.

Send everyone to the CRM pushes contacts to GoHighLevel. It is safe to re-run — anyone already contacted has their thread backfilled and is never emailed again — and Preview shows you what it would do first.

Communication, acquisition and support

Talking to people, and who is asking

17

Inbox

Go to www.tryjalla.com/admin/inbox

Every conversation — WhatsApp, email and platform messages — in one place, with a Needs reply count that is the same number the Overview shows. The sidebar's WhatsApp, Jalla Messages, Email and Calls entries are all filtered views of this one page, not separate inboxes.

WhatsApp is live in production and round-trips: a client's message arrives here, and your reply reaches their phone. A thread can be linked to a project so the conversation sits with the build it concerns.

With nothing yet it says “A thread appears here the moment a client writes to Groundwork. Nothing is shown until then.”

18

Contractor Applications

Go to www.tryjalla.com/admin/applications

Founding-network applications, filtered by status — All, Pending, Reviewing, Accepted, Rejected, Disqualified. A banner flags anyone who was never told their application arrived, so the backlog is visible rather than remembered.

Open one to see the full submission: contact details, trade, experience, credentials and uploaded documents (through signed links), project history with client references, and the standards they accepted. From there you can set status, send the decision email, re-send the acknowledgement, push to the CRM, and edit the application — corrections are stamped with who made them and when.

Accepting publishes them to the public contractor directory. That is the only route in.

19

Support

Go to www.tryjalla.com/admin/support

Messages from the help form and account-deletion requests. Every ticket also emails the team inbox when it arrives, so this page is the record rather than the only alert.

Each ticket carries the sender, their email, the date, the message, and a status you can move through Open → In progress → Resolved → Closed. “Open” in the counts means open and in progress — everything still owed to someone.

Deletion requests are flagged separately with a 48-hour promise made to the sender.

System

The record, and the gaps

20

Audit log

Go to www.tryjalla.com/admin/audit-log

Everything Groundwork recorded, newest first: who did what, on which project, and when. Stage approvals, evidence uploads, assignments, budget changes.

The line that matters is on the page: “Written by the database itself — nothing here can be edited from a browser.” Staff cannot alter it, which is what makes it worth anything in a dispute.

21

Pages not built yet

Four menu items open an honest placeholder: Inspections, Tasks, Site Managers and Agents. The page says the section is part of the admin but its page is not built, and promises that when it ships everything on it will come from real data.

These are not bugs. They are in the menu because the information architecture is settled even though the pages are not, and a dead link would be worse than an honest one.

Also present and real, but not given their own entry in this guide: Started applications (abandoned drafts), Waitlist, Quote requests, CRM (GoHighLevel sync state and backlog), Notifications, Analytics, Integrations, Automation requests, Team & permissions and Settings.

Before you report a bug

Two things that look like faults and are not

22

What admin deliberately cannot do

Some absences are designed. Groundwork's value is that the record is trustworthy, and that requires staff to be limited in specific ways:

You cannotBecause
Record a verificationOnly the assigned verifier does that. You approve the stage afterwards; the finding is theirs.
Edit the audit logThe database writes it. A record staff can edit proves nothing.
Hold or move client moneyGroundwork records and authorises; a licensed provider holds and moves it.
Submit site evidence as a contractorEvidence is filed by the person who did the work; a verifier is explicitly refused too.
Enter /work or /verifiersBeing an admin is not being a contractor or verifier. Manage them from the People pages instead.
Issue a certificate on Self VerifyCertificates require independent verification. Self Verify has none by definition.
23

Empty, zero and unavailable are three different things

The admin is careful to distinguish them, and the difference is usually the answer to “is this broken?”

You seeIt means
0A real count that is genuinely zero. Nothing is wrong.
An empty state with a sentenceRead fine, there is nothing there — usually with the reason, e.g. “Accept an application to add one.”
“Not available” or a dashThe source could not be read. This one is worth reporting.
“Nothing recorded yet”The project exists but has no entries of this kind — different from a zero balance.
If you see a row you cannot explain, that is the bug

The admin shows only real Supabase and GoHighLevel data. There are no sample projects, placeholder figures or demo threads anywhere in it. So a project you do not recognise, a number that cannot be sourced, or a person who does not exist is worth reporting immediately — the rule is that it should be impossible.
