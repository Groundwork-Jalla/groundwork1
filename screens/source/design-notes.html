Contractor dashboard

Rebuilt around the one thing a contractor actually does on this platform: stand on a site, on a phone, and prove a stage is finished. Everything else is arranged to stay out of the way of that.

What changed and why. The current screens are a list of tabs that each answer a different question. This replaces them with one spine — the ten-stage pipeline, horizontally, the same shape the client sees — and hangs the work off the stage a contractor is standing in.
Evidence is the product. A substage is not ticked by hand. It ticks when a file lands against it. That removes the gap where work is done, the box is ticked, and nothing was ever uploaded — which is the gap verification exists to close.

No "needs action" tab. The pipeline already says what needs doing — the open dot is the work. A separate task list would be a second copy of the same truth, free to disagree with it.

Why the pipeline leads

A contractor's first question is never "what tasks do I have" — it is "where is this build and what releases next". The strip answers both in one glance, and it is the same strip the client is looking at, so a phone call about progress starts from a shared picture.

Money sits under the stage, not in a separate tab. The figure that matters is the one attached to the stage being worked, in the project's own currency with the dollar equivalent beside it — the same pairing now used in the wizard.
Ten dots, always. They never scroll or collapse. A contractor learns the shape of a build once and reads every project the same way after that. Labels are abbreviated to fit a phone: land secured, design completed, site preparation, foundation, structure & walls, roofing, electrical & plumbing, finishing, exterior work, final handover — the ten stages the platform already runs on.

The upload rule

One file against one substage. The tick, the client's notification and the stage's readiness for verification all follow from that single act, so there is no second step to forget.

Offline is normal. A site in Limbe loses signal. The upload queues, the row shows it queued, and it sends when the phone has a bar again — the contractor does not stand in the road holding their phone up.
Nothing here approves anything. Uploading evidence is a claim, not a decision. Verification and approval stay where they are, with the verifier.

Open questions

Three things this proposal does not settle, flagged rather than hidden:

1 · Substages per stage. The four shown are illustrative. The real list comes from project_substages, and whether a contractor may add one on site is undecided.
2 · Who sees Money. Shown here as the contractor's own earnings. If an invited client opens the same project they must not see this tab in these words.
3 · Desktop. This is drawn for the phone, which is where the work happens. The wide layout is the same spine with the stage list and its evidence side by side — worth agreeing before it is built, not after.
